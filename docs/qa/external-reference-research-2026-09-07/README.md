# External references for Sonoite

Research date: 7 September 2026. Scope: reference research and documentation. No application source, configuration or builds changed in this pass.

The strongest combination is **UVA for light as a performer, KID A MNESIA for turning artwork into an environment, and Max Cooper for distinct visual ideas across an album**. Opera North is the closest functioning browser reference inspected. These offer ways to give the stage more responsibility alongside the camera.

## What was actually checked

- **The Turn of the Screw:** loaded the live WebGL experience, chose Medium quality, observed running playback and the Night scene, then selected Day for a diagnostic comparison. The environment and chapter controls worked. This was sampling, not an uninterrupted viewing of the whole trailer.
- **The Boat:** launched the live experience and inspected its opening plus two labelled scroll samples. Its illustrated storm, layered foreground/background and later quiet panel composition rendered. The automatic-scroll control was visible but not tested.
- **Over the Hills:** the live gramophone entry scene rendered. The needle interaction, complete musical sequence and audio positioning were not validated.
- **UVA Our Time 1x3:** opened the artist's documentation and its embedded official film. Playback advanced; inspected a sample near 87 seconds and a paused diagnostic at 24 seconds. This was not a visit to the installation or a complete film review.
- **KID A MNESIA, Unspoken Words, Aether, Through the Dark and Just a Reflektor:** primary creator/publisher documentation. No native exhibition or full album film was played. Through the Dark's legacy launch could not be verified by this browser research; that does not establish that it is retired. Google's page explicitly marks Just a Reflektor inactive.

I cannot hear browser audio through these tools. Statements about intended audio mapping below come from credited creators, not an audition. Attention judgments are design hypotheses based on the inspected material, not measured audience retention. Some tabs became background tabs during research; none of the samples is represented as continuous foreground playback.

## Prioritised reference set

### 1. UVA — Our Time / Our Time 1x3

**Type:** physical audiovisual installation; official film available. **Best use:** Track 1's travelling light and Track 4's fixture.

UVA describes independently moving pendulums carrying light and sound. The 2023 configuration uses a score by Mira Calix. In the inspected film frames, illumination locates people and patches of floor inside a much darker volume; the source has a physical position. [Artist documentation and film](https://www.uva.co.uk/features/our-time-2023), [original installation](https://www.uva.co.uk/features/our-time).

**Borrow:** position, direction, acceleration, pause and occlusion as lighting events. Let one source alter relationships between sculpture, cloth and wall. **Avoid:** copying pendulum arrays, bright floor circles or installation-scale darkness. A visitor inhabits that darkness; Sonoite must communicate through a screen.

**Sonoite application:** a fixed camera watches the key approach a fold, lose part of the sculpture behind it, then expose a displaced wall shadow. Physical causality supplies the event. Track 4 can retain its stationary failing lamp; the substantial change belongs to the shadow it casts.

### 2. Radiohead — KID A MNESIA EXHIBITION

**Type:** native virtual exhibition; production case study, not a browser demo. **Best use:** the album artwork becoming the space.

The creators describe rooms built around the source artwork and audio concept, with projections, illuminated surfaces and video sources affecting room light. The Paper Chamber turns sketchbook material into architecture. They also describe deliberately planned lighting transitions and room identities. [Creator interview published by Unreal Engine](https://www.unrealengine.com/spotlights/creating-kid-a-mnesia-an-exclusive-look-at-radiohead-s-new-virtual-exhibit?lang=en).

**Borrow:** enlarge a material or visual relationship already present in the artwork until it defines the environment. **Avoid:** importing its pyramids, forests, television stacks or particle paintings. Its spatial music arrangements also involved production resources beyond a mastered stereo file.

**Sonoite application:** the cover's compressed cloth, negative space and suspended oval can determine the room's folds, gaps and weight. A drape should imply a covered volume or tension across something unseen, with a recognisable contour. The current hanging receiver is a starting point, not the finished artwork translation.

### 3. Max Cooper — Unspoken Words

**Type:** audiovisual album and companion website. **Best use:** distinct tracks belonging to one larger progression.

Cooper commissioned a sequence of 13 films around the music's emotional and structural ideas. The companion site explains those decisions chapter by chapter. For example, Growth connects bodily expansion with a central musical pulse and its spatial treatment. [Album project](https://www.maxcooper.net/unspoken-words), [companion site](https://unspokenwords.net/), [Growth chapter](https://unspokenwords.net/growth/).

**Borrow:** one legible visual process per piece, with development across the album. Give the music a visual analogy more specific than amplitude. **Avoid:** adopting every film's visual language or attaching a literal psychological story to Sonoite that its owner has not chosen.

**Sonoite application:** Track 1 changes visibility through light; Track 4 changes the room through electrical failure; Track 5 changes matter through contamination. Their motion density, surface response and ending should differ even when the grey sculpture remains shared.

### 4. Opera North / Lusion — The Turn of the Screw

**Type:** functioning interactive browser trailer. **Best use:** an audio-led environment with optional exploration.

The project combines spatial recordings, James Bulley's sound work and Lusion's WebGL environment. The live page offers a continuous trailer plus chapter selection and viewpoint interaction. In the sampled Day/Night views, the landscape remains recognisable while its lighting and atmosphere change strongly. [Live experience](https://www.operanorth.co.uk/turn-of-the-screw-immersive-trailer/), [creator case study](https://lusion.co/projects/the_turn_of_the_screw/).

**Borrow:** spatial continuity through changing conditions, readable chapter identities and a passive viewing path. **Avoid:** its glowing particles, coloured fog, landscape literalism and putting required exploration between the listener and the music.

**Sonoite application:** keep a familiar arrangement of sculpture, receiving surface and floor while visibility changes across the score. Foreground occlusion can imply depth before the whole chamber opens. This is a visual proposal; it does not require remixing Sonoite into binaural audio.

### 5. SBS / Matt Huynh — The Boat

**Type:** functioning illustrated browser narrative; not an album visualiser. **Best use:** atmosphere and pacing without modelling every element in 3D.

Huynh's source artwork was developed into an interactive narrative with animation and sound design. The sampled opening combines ink textures, rain, water and tilting panels; a later sample gives space to much quieter faces and white paper. This establishes a visible change in density. [Live experience](https://www.sbs.com.au/theboat/), [artist's project and process](https://www.matthuynh.com/stories/theboat-9rw43).

**Borrow:** a consistent image-making technique, layered depth and relief after density. **Avoid:** copying rain, camera rocking, constant reading or the work's refugee narrative.

**Sonoite application:** use carefully authored textile detail, shadow plates or filmed material studies where they outperform generic procedural geometry. A hybrid treatment is worth testing for close inserts; it need not replace the interactive sculpture. Preserve depth/occlusion, and compare under the actual dark grade.

### 6. Hilltop Hoods / Google / R/GA — Through the Dark

**Type:** legacy interactive music film; live launch unverified here. **Best use:** authored cinema with bounded interaction.

The published project combines contrasting light/dark worlds with camera control through device motion or desktop input. Scenes were authored in Maya and rendered in the browser; lighting was deliberately designed. [Publisher's project record](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/through-the-dark).

**Borrow:** author the scene and its musical progression before offering limited variation in how it is seen. **Avoid:** phone gymnastics, permanent input demands, or transferring the film's binary emotional symbolism to this album.

**Sonoite application:** the full film should remain compelling with no pointer activity. If optional inspection is introduced later, constrain it to a small view or focus range and return gently to the score. There is no need to add this interaction to the current implementation.

### 7. McBess / Ultranoir / The Dead Pirates — Over the Hills

**Type:** live browser music experience; entry scene inspected. **Best use:** cohesive artwork across object, stage and interface.

The project places McBess's illustrations and music around an interactive gramophone, using Three.js and Web Audio. The visible entry is largely monochrome, with graphic patterning carried across the object and its surroundings. The gramophone's shape and material regions read immediately. [Live experience and project credits](https://www.overthetinyhills.com/).

**Borrow:** material/value separation and consistency of visual language, even in a stylised room. **Avoid:** its cartoon props, persistent controls and novelty interactions. The functioning entry is not proof that every older interaction still works.

**Sonoite application:** preserve the useful grey treatment but give stage materials distinct scales of detail: broad cloth folds, restrained wall texture and wet floor response. More meshes alone will not provide that differentiation.

### 8. Max Cooper / Architecture Social Club — Aether

**Type:** physical installation; creator documentation. **Best use:** thinking of light as a volume rather than a brightness slider.

The creators describe a three-dimensional light field mapped to an immersive audio experience, forming waves, surfaces and spatial structures around the audience. [Project and official film](https://www.maxcooper.net/aether).

**Borrow:** coherent movement across a substantial region of space. **Avoid:** reproducing the luminous lattice; it would risk returning to the dotted geometry already rejected in Sonoite.

**Sonoite application:** test a continuous, dim patch of depth where light meets cloth or a wall, with a consistent direction and boundary. Judge whether it exposes distance or simply washes out the image. This could inform Tracks 1/5; Track 4 retains its zero-fog premise.

### 9. Arcade Fire / Vincent Morisset — Just a Reflektor

**Type:** archived interactive film; explicitly inactive. **Best use:** effects that alter how an image is revealed.

The project used a mobile device and webcam to control virtual projection over a film. Google's surviving description also documents its effects editor and open-source implementation. [Publisher's archive](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/just-a-reflektor).

**Borrow:** treat an effect as a change to perception of the photographed subject. **Avoid:** required device pairing or effects becoming the principal attraction. This is a historical reference, not a working launch recommendation.

**Sonoite application:** a shaped light interruption, narrow reflection or moment of obscuration can change the meaning of a familiar composition without introducing another decorative object. Keep the gesture authored and optional interaction unnecessary.

## What this changes about our direction

These are my proposed translations, not claims that the reference creators would endorse them.

The recent Track 1 study already has a travelling light, a real shadow receiver and a fold that settles once. Recommending those again would not advance the work. Its next improvement is to compose how these systems meet. The [current study note](../shadow-drape-2026-09-07/README.md) records the receiver's 125-second entrance and the single settlement across 316.65–365 seconds; it explicitly does not add a new phrase-based light score.

| Track | Strongest next development | What would be visibly different | Keep from the accepted direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 — Sonoite | Choreograph light position, interruption and shadow readability together | A neutral key approaches an edge, loses part of its path behind a fold, then reveals the sculpture's enlarged shadow in a newly legible place. A pause lets the viewer register the change. | Grey sculpture, close inserts, grounded dim drape, later wall extinction |
| 4 — Horas mortas | Make the fixture's existing behaviour affect a substantial receiving surface | Each strike reveals a specific hard shadow and a patch of worn material; the stable core remains spatially steady; the final loss removes those cues in an intelligible order. | Sodium, credible mounting, authored failing-light cadence, zero fog |
| 5 — Não querer nada | Improve the connection between material change and its surroundings | In a recurring medium view, a broad wet reflection and irregular contact residue make the altered place legible. The accepted close-up explains what caused it. | Accepted tar-frontier cut, rare surface gestures, permanent contamination |

This is compatible with additional stage elements. A covered asymmetrical volume, a tensioned fold, an unseen light-blocking edge or a reflective contact surface can earn a place because it changes something else. Each needs a clear beginning, consequence and resting state. A loop that continues forever gives little sense of elapsed time.

For ambience, I would judge each layer by **what spatial information it adds**. Haze should reveal a depth interval. Cloth should imply weight and concealment. A shadow should identify a relationship between source, object and receiver. Surface detail should survive the final camera distance and grade. These are stronger criteria than a fixed percentage of black or an arbitrary number of props.

## Technical implications for a later implementation

- Keep large stage changes on the recording-time score. Use the live analyser only for small modulation inside those authored limits. The references do not validate replacing the existing timing with a new generic onset detector.
- Keep a matched-camera comparison. If the light/room version does not communicate a change while the camera is held, another cut can conceal the problem.
- Evaluate actual material authoring alongside code: unequal folds, contact, fine roughness and receiver geometry. Procedural motion cannot supply missing construction quality.
- Try a pre-rendered or photographed material insert only as an explicit comparison. The Boat supports the value of coherent authored imagery; it does not prove that a flat plate will integrate with Sonoite's moving light.
- Spatial-audio references are inspiration for causal staging. Their audio arrangements are not a reason to spatialise or modify Sonoite's stereo masters without a separate musical decision.

## Top five things to keep

1. The cover's tactile vocabulary and the grey sculpture's readability in darkness.
2. Light, shadow and receiving surfaces acting as one physical relationship.
3. Distinct timescales: slow development, sparse phrase events and quiet rests.
4. Recognisable returning compositions that reveal an irreversible change.
5. Complete passive playback; optional controls serve the listener.

## Top five things to cut or resist

1. Anonymous glow balls, dotted grids and haze layers that expose their own construction.
2. Sending the same audio envelope to every visual channel.
3. Asking camera movement to compensate for an unchanged stage.
4. Copying an installation's darkness without its physical scale and presence.
5. Adding further objects before their material, contact and consequence can be seen.

## Top five next moves, in order

1. **Track 1 light score:** compare the present sweep with an approach–occlusion–reveal–rest sequence under the same held camera. Accept only if the event is readable without a cut or a global brightness jump. Placement remains subject to the actual recording and owner audition.
2. **Track 1 receiver quality:** compare the current hanging surface with one tensioned or covered-volume form derived from the cover. The added contour must communicate weight and remain subordinate to the sculpture. Do this after evaluating the light sequence, not simultaneously.
3. **Track 4 room consequence:** inspect matched lit/unlit states around existing strikes. The fixture, its mounting and the shadow should belong to the same geometry; do not introduce fog to make the lamp legible.
4. **Track 5 wet aftermath:** refine the existing direction so a final medium view reads as changed matter and contact, not merely lower exposure. Preserve the accepted close-up and compare the beginning/end at the same framing.
5. **Sustained viewing:** after those bounded comparisons, watch normal-speed passages and then complete films with the owner. A pleasing still, primary-source description or shader check cannot decide boredom or musical timing.
